# Open Nextion ONX2432G028, 320 × 240

This is the **Open Nextion ONX2432G028** (2.8-inch "Genius" board, hardware V1.3): a 240 x 320 panel with an ST7789
driver over SPI (ESPHome's `mipi_spi` model `ST7789V`) and **CST826 capacitive touch** over I2C (ESPHome's `cst816`
platform, ESPHome 2025.12.0 or newer), on an ESP32-S3R8 with 16 MB flash and 8 MB of octal PSRAM. A PCF8574A
expander at 0x38 carries the display's reset.

**Experimental: the profile builds, and has not been on the glass yet.** The pins come from the vendor's own
examples ([OpenNextion-SKU-ONX2432G028](https://github.com/OpenNextion/OpenNextion-SKU-ONX2432G028), ESP-IDF
`01_touch_test`) and its openHASP port (`user_setups/esp32s3/onx2432g028.ini`).

It is not a CYD, though the glass has the same size: another chip, another display controller, capacitive touch.
The CYD profile does not run on it.

## Pins

| Part | Pin |
|---|---|
| Display SCLK / MOSI | GPIO5 / GPIO1 |
| Display CS / DC | GPIO2 / GPIO3 |
| Display reset | PCF8574 P5 (`LCD_RESET_EXIO`) |
| Backlight | GPIO6, PWM |
| I2C SDA / SCL | GPIO8 / GPIO7: touch (0x15), expander (0x38), RTC, the Grove I2C socket |
| Touch interrupt | PCF8574 P4, not on a pin of the chip: the touch is polled |

The expander, numbered from zero as the vendor's wiki does: P0 speaker enable, P1 RTC interrupt, P2 charging,
P3 camera power down, P4 touch interrupt, P5 display reset, P6 SD card chip select.

Spare for your own hardware (docs/EXTRA_HARDWARE.md): the Grove UART1 socket (GPIO13 TX, GPIO12 RX) and the 24-pin
header, as long as the camera and the microphone it shares its pins with stay unused.

## Layout and capabilities

- Landscape: 320 x 240, two columns of three cells. Portrait: 240 x 320, one column of four cells. The compact
  look at 143 dpi: the CYD's glass, so the CYD's sizes.
- With PSRAM: camera pictures, snapshots, and 128 tiles on 24 pages.
- The backlight is a direct PWM pin: dimming, standby and night mode.
- The battery connector, the SD card, the speaker, the microphone and the camera socket are not used.

## What the first flash has to settle

1. **The reset line.** The vendor's ESP-IDF driver and wiki make it P5; the vendor's Arduino example pulses P6. If
   the glass stays dark or shows noise, set `LCD_RESET_EXIO: "6"` in the screen's Override YAML.
2. **Colours.** `DISPLAY_INVERT_COLORS` (`false`) and `DISPLAY_COLOR_ORDER` (`rgb`) follow the vendor's ESP-IDF
   example. Complementary colours: invert. Red and blue swapped: `bgr`.
3. **Mirroring and touch direction.** The vendor's Arduino example mirrors the picture in X, its ESP-IDF example
   does not. Check the four corners lying down and standing up; `TOUCH_MIRROR_X`, `TOUCH_MIRROR_Y` and
   `TOUCH_SWAP_XY` turn the touch.
4. **Touch at the edges.** A review (CNX Software, March 2026) found a strip along one side of this glass where a
   touch is not seen, without saying which side or how wide. A page swipe starts in a band along the left or right
   edge (`EDGE_SWIPE_BAND_PX`, 27 px here, about 5 mm): if the dead strip is on one of those sides the way the
   screen hangs, widen the band or hang it the other way. The chevrons of the page bar change the page either way.
5. **The data rate.** 40 MHz to start with; the vendor runs the display at 80 MHz (`DISPLAY_DATA_RATE`).
6. **The backlight.** 10 kHz, as the vendor's openHASP port; if dimming is uneven, try `BACKLIGHT_FREQUENCY: "1000Hz"`.
